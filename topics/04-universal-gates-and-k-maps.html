Concept 1 of 4

Universal gates

A universal gate can build any Boolean function alone. NAND and NOR are universal, and they are cheap and easy to fabricate, so real chips use them heavily.

  • NAND: NOT = tie the inputs together. AND = NAND followed by that NOT. OR = A+B = (A′B′)′, a NAND of inverted inputs.
  • NOR: NOT = tie the inputs together. OR = NOR followed by NOT. AND = AB = (A′+B′)′, a NOR of inverted inputs.

An AND-OR-INVERT (AOI) circuit for a sum of products becomes NAND-NAND: F = AB + CD = ((AB)′(CD)′)′. Using one gate type reduces the number of different ICs you need.

Concept 2 of 4

Karnaugh maps

A K-map is a truth table rearranged so that neighbouring cells differ in exactly one variable. Rows and columns are labelled in Gray-code order (00, 01, 11, 10), not binary order. A 2-variable map has 4 cells, 3 variables 8, and 4 variables 16. Each cell is one minterm.

Concept 3 of 4

Grouping rules

  • Groups contain 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 cells (a power of 2) and are rectangular.
  • No diagonals, and never include a cell of the wrong value.
  • Make each group as large as possible and use as few groups as possible.
  • Groups may overlap.
  • Groups may wrap around: left and right edges are neighbours, top and bottom too, so the four corners form a valid group.

In each group, variables that change value are eliminated. To get SOP, group the 1s and write a product term per group. To get POS, group the 0s and write a sum term per group using the complemented literals.

Concept 4 of 4

Prime implicants and don’t-cares

A prime implicant is a group that cannot grow any larger. An essential prime implicant covers at least one 1 that no other prime implicant covers, so it must appear in the answer. Pick all essential groups first, then cover any remaining 1s with as few extra groups as possible.

A don’t-care (X or d) is an input combination that never occurs or whose output does not matter. Treat it as 1 when that enlarges a group, otherwise ignore it. Never form a group made only of X cells.

Worked example 1 of 2

Worked example 1

Simplify F(A,B,C,D) = Σm(1,3,7,11,15) + d(0,2,5).
Show the solution
  1. CD covers 3, 7, 11, 15 and is essential (only it covers 11).
  2. Cell 1 is still uncovered. A′B′ (0,1,2,3) or A′D (1,3,5,7) both cover it.
  3. Result F = CD + A′B′ (or CD + A′D). Both are minimal.

Worked example 2 of 2

Worked example 2

Simplify F(x,y,z) = Σm(2,3,4,5).
Show the solution
  1. Cells 2 and 3 (x′y) form a group. Cells 4 and 5 (xy′) form another.
  2. F = x′y + xy′, which is x ⊕ y.

Show the answer
A′B′ + BC′ + AC (or A′C′ + B′C + AB). Both have three terms.

Summary

Summary

  • NAND and NOR are universal. Tie inputs for NOT.
  • K-map order is Gray code: 00, 01, 11, 10.
  • Groups are 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 cells, may overlap, and may wrap.
  • Take essential prime implicants first, then cover what is left.
  • Use don’t-cares only when they make a group larger.
